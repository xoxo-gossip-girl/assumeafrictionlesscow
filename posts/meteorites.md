# Meteorites
### _By Mooshroom_
### What is a meteorite?
A **meteorite** is a rock that survives passing through a planet's atmosphere and lands on the surface.

**It is made of:** mostly silicate minerals, iron, nickel and basalt.

**Formation:** The rocks originate form the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

Collisions between different objects in the belt cause asteroids to be broken apart and sent on different trajectories, some of which enter into Earth's orbit and fall to the surface.

### Different types of meteorites

**1. Stony meteorites:** over 95% of all meteorites
- Made of metal and silicates
- Dull and stone-like, dark in colour and heavy
- Often underrepresented due to similarities to Earth rocks
- Some have small metal grains called chondrules. These meteorites are called chondrites.

**2. Stony-iron meteorites:** less than 1% of all meteorites
- Made of metal and silicate
- Formed from boundary metal between iron and nickel core and rocky mantle of asteroids so contain both.

**3. Iron meteorites:** around 5% of all meteorites
- Made of mostly iron and nickel from cores of asteroids
- Very dense and shiny from the high heat and pressure.
- Easier to identify as they are magnetic and shiny.
- Have Widmanstätten patterns due to long cooling time

### Meteors, meteoroids, meteorites, comets and asteroids

**Comet:** A chunk of ice and rock originating from the outer solar system, often accompanied by a coma and tail.
**Asteroid:** A rock in orbit (usually between Mars and Jupiter)
**Meteoroid:** A rock in orbit that is smaller than an asteroid.
**Meteor:** A space rock that is entering and burning up in the atmosphere.
**Meteorite:** A space rock that did not completely burn up in the atmosphere and has landed on Earth.

### Widmanstätten patterns

**Widmanstätten patterns** are a pattern of lines that occur inside of iron meteorites.

They only occur in iron meteorites as they are formed in the cores of asteroids, where there is more heat, as a very slow rate of cooling is required for their formation.

The required rate of cooling is about **1-100°C per million years.**

As the meteorites cool over millions of years inside of asteroids, the patterns are formed.

The pattern is the crystals of two different alloys of iron and nickel: **kamacite** and **taenite.**

As the meteorite slowly cools, the alloys form at different rates, causing the interlocking grid pattern.

Analysing these patterns can tell us how quickly the meteorite cooled, which helps in identifying which asteroid it came from.

These patterns are not formed on Earth as Earth cools too fast, meaning the cooling time is not long enough for these patterns to occur.

These patterns can be revealed by taking a cross-section of an iron meteorite, polishing the surface and adding a weak acid. The acid etches way the kamacite, and the taenite is left behind.

### How much mass of meteorite material falls onto Earth every day?

Approximately **44,000kg** of meteorite material...

… but only a small portion of the mass is made up of larger meteorites.

The majority of this is **microscopic particles** of meteor material that break off when shooting stars burn up in the atmosphere due to the high speed and heat at entry. They can also come from asteroid collisions and leftover material from the formation of the early Solar System.

### How I found (micro)meteorites

**How I identified them:**
- Usually between 100 and 400 micrometers in diameter
- Round due to the heat and surface tension as they melt on entry into the atmosphere.
- Shiny due to the melting process.
- Using a strong magnet and a plastic bag, I searched on a roof for any magnetic material. I ensured that the location was far from a construction site and that it had rained recently, so it is unlikely that my findings were industrial material.
